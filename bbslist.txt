BBSes that answered
===================

Dialled from the dial-up list at telnetbbsguide.com through a
voip.ms trunk (G.711) with modec. Twenty-five numbers were called;
these twenty answered. The other five -- ARDA-BBS, Level 29, Moe's
Tavern, Neo-Vintage BBS and Wizard's Rainbow -- never picked up.

Connected  = modulations modec actually established with.

             every modulation in Table 4/V.8. Blank means it never

Menus below came back under a restricted offer (only the modes modec
can run were advertised), so they say what the far end has in common
with modec, not everything it has:

KEEP BBS:
    data, V.22bis/V.22

No V.8 menu at all: C64 Pub, Dark Realms, Last Telegraph Office, Never Never Land, Particles BBS, Sursum Corda, TuxedoCat Lounge.

V.21 was attempted against the seven boards whose V.8 menu listed it,
originating on channel 1 (980/1180 Hz) with no V.8. Four answered in
V.21 and passed a banner:

    Digital Distortion    CONNECT 300, full Synchronet banner
    End Of The Line       CONNECT 300, banner offering Terminal or Viewdata
    ENiAC 2.0             CONNECT 300, ANSI banner
    Reign of Fire         CONNECT 300, carrier up, no banner before hangup

Basement BBS and Phoenix BBS answered and never put a carrier up against
V.21 tones; Kludge BBS did not answer at all. So a menu listing V.21 is a
statement about the modem's capability, not a promise that its answer
side is configured for it -- which is what a US-firmware modem left on
ATB1 looks like from the outside.

V.23 duplex was tried against End Of The Line, the one board that offers
viewdata and lists V.23 duplex in its V.8 menu. Offered under V.8 it was
accepted -- the JM came back "data, V.23 duplex" -- and then nothing
followed: the far end went silent after CJ and never put up a forward
carrier. Offered without V.8 it walked its ordinary ladder instead,
2250 Hz unscrambled binary 1 for three seconds and then V.21 channel 2,
with no 1300 Hz rung anywhere in it. So the menu entry is the modem's
capability list rather than its answer ladder, and the board's Viewdata
option is a terminal mode reached over an ordinary connection, not a V.23
line. V.23 duplex works end to end in the simulator; it has yet to be
confirmed against a real board, and the boards that would answer it are
European.

Bell 212A was attempted against twelve of these and connected with
none. Their fallback ladder runs ANSam, then V.22's 2250 Hz
unscrambled binary 1, with a Bell 103 rung below it on some; the
Bell 212A answer sequence is not offered at all.
