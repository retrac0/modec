Dial-up targets found online
============================

Survey of what still answers a modem in 2026, beyond the twenty BBSes in
bbslist.txt.

2. NIST Automated Computer Time Service (ACTS)
-----------------------------------------------

    +13034944774    NIST Boulder, Colorado

Still operating: the NIST page was updated February 2026. Up to 9600
baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit; at least 1200 baud is needed to
receive the full time code.

This is the most useful target on the list, and not because of what it
is. Every other system here sends a banner nobody can predict, so a
decode can only be judged by whether it looks like English. ACTS sends a
documented, fixed-format ASCII time code, once a second, forever. That
makes it an oracle: the expected bytes are known in advance, so bit error
rate can be measured against them rather than guessed at. It is also a
public service whose entire purpose is to be dialled by a computer.

Called once. It answered after 8.7 s and modec connected at V.22
1200 bit/s in 3.8 s -- and then delivered this:

    L3hbljrY>jS8vrTEMe"56c@Fx5"B|BoPV,zN5FRf<Ak?vG"{}OO#'Zk+"4#%AVwS...
    UUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUU
    NO CARRIER

Not a time code. That string starts with hbljrY>jS8vrTEMe, the same
sequence five BBSes returned, which is this end's descrambler running on
an idle carrier: the framer arms and starts delivering bytes before the
far end has begun sending. The run of U characters is the descrambler
settling on a constant.

This is what the oracle is for. Against a BBS the same failure is only a
suspicion -- the banner might genuinely look like that. Against ACTS
there is no doubt at all: the bytes were supposed to be a date and a
time, and they were not. The bug is reproducible on demand, on a service
that never changes, without needing anyone's BBS.

Correction, 6 September 2026. "The framer arms before the far end has
begun sending" does not survive the recording. Taking the call apart
symbol by symbol -- modec's own receiver dumping its phase decisions,
descrambled by hand -- ACTS is transmitting: entropy in the descrambled
bit stream sits at 50 % from 13 to 15 s, and after that the line is idle
mark with a short burst of data every second, which is exactly the shape
of a time code sent once a second. The far end was talking and we did
not understand it. Whatever is wrong is downstream of the symbol
decisions, and it is not an arming problem, so the fix is not the one
this section assumed.

Two things it is not. The V.22 receive path is sound in general: modec
calls spandsp's V.22bis modem over a pair of FIFOs and text passes
intact both ways at 1200 bit/s (SURVEY.md has the oracle recipe). And
the scrambler is not per-direction: V.22 uses 1 + x^-14 + x^-17 in both
directions, which the same spandsp reference confirms.

4. FidoNet nodelist -- surveyed
--------------------------------

nodelist.249 in the repository root is the Z2DAILY for Sunday
6 September 2026. 1233 entries: 391 in zone 1 (North America), 759 in
zone 2 (Europe), 39 in zone 3, 44 in zone 4. Zones 3 and 4 publish no
telephone number at all. Zone 2 publishes 107 and is set aside here.

Zone 1 publishes 35 node entries, which collapse to 20 distinct numbers:
coordinators, gateways and hubs routinely list the same sysop's line
under several node numbers.

Four of the twenty are already in bbslist.txt, and the nodelist has all
four exactly right -- Dark Realms 647-847-2083, Phoenix BBS
860-446-6118, Sursum Corda 256-895-4786, Moe's Tavern 502-875-8938.
Four for four is the useful part: the phone field is being maintained
rather than left to rot, so the other sixteen are worth dialling.

None of the twenty is flagged Down, Hold or Pvt.

Nodes reachable only by telephone -- no INA, IBN, IFC or ITN flag of any
kind, so a modem is the only way in and the line has to be live for the
node to still be in the list:

    1-703-777-8383  Leesburg VA      Lincoln Legacy         HST V32B V42B
    1-817-489-5063  Fort Worth TX    The Black Knights BBS  V32B V34 VFC V42B
    1-301-949-5764  Wheaton MD       The Idea Link (+2)     V32B V42
    1-703-310-7439  Vienna VA        bbs.cyberchatnet.com   V32B V42

These four are the strongest candidates in this whole document. Lincoln
Legacy's HST flag means a USR Courier at the far end, which is the modem
modec has the most to learn from: it answers HST, V.32bis, V.22bis,
V.22, V.23, V.21 and Bell 103/212A depending on what it hears.

Nodes with both modem flags and an internet address -- the mailer traffic

    1-209-785-4189  Copperopolis CA  The Wastelands BBS     V34 VFC V42B
    1-559-429-8280  Visalia CA       US 99 BBS              V32B

Nodes that publish a number but no modem capability flags at all. The
number may be a voice line or a leftover; lowest priority:

    1-201-601-4011  Old Bridge NJ    ASCIIattic
    1-724-552-9698  New Derry PA     The Titantic BBS
    1-931-494-9100  Franklin WI      Reign Of Fire BBS
    1-936-333-6077  Houston TX       InterMail Help
    1-484-784-9994  Coatesville PA   Pioneer's Station      MO

Two notes on that last group. Reign of Fire is already in bbslist.txt at
423-541-8271, where it answered and connected at V.22; 931-494-9100 is a
second line for the same board, so it is a same-system comparison rather
than a new one. Pioneer's Station carries MO, mail only: it will answer
and handshake but there is no human side to it, so it is a handshake
sample and nothing else.

Where an entry above shows 300 in the nodelist baud field it is the
nodelist default left untouched by a sysop whose node is IP-only; it is
not a claim of 300 bit/s service.

On V.21: every modem flagged here is V.32bis or later, and boxes of that
generation -- Courier, ZyXEL, Supra -- carry the full ITU set including
V.21, so this is the first realistic chance to exercise modec's V.21 half
in North America. The catch is which end picks. A US-firmware modem
answering defaults to Bell 103 (ATB1) and only answers V.21 tones when
the sysop has set ATB0 or a non-US country profile. So originate V.21,
expect a Bell 103 answer as the ordinary outcome, and treat any board
that comes back at 1650/1850 Hz as the find.

6. Boards that still run at 300 bit/s
--------------------------------------

modec implements V.21 (v21Channel1/v21Channel2 in Standards.hs) and has
never once used it against a real system: every 300 bit/s connection in
bbslist.txt is Bell 103, because every system in it is North American.
These three are European boards that advertise 300 bit/s, so they should
answer with V.21's 1650/1850 Hz pair, and they are the only way to find
out whether that half of the FSK code works outside the bench.

    300 baud-BBS      +49 39424 940818    300/8/N/1, TTY
    SnobSoft BBS      +49 40 609 43485    300 baud, 7N1, ASCII
    TECS Hamburg      +49 40 6441 3888

300 baud-BBS is run by the efb-1 computer museum specifically so that
people can point acoustic couplers at something. The software is IBBS, a
BASIC board from 1982; file transfer is disabled; there is no compression
and no error correction, which is exactly what we want -- nothing between
our demodulator and the bytes. Its page was updated 11/2024 with a log of
the hardware callers have used, so the sysop is still reading the board.
It is the closest thing to a second oracle after NIST ACTS: not a known
plaintext, but a known-slow, known-plain line.

It is V.21, said in as many words on the museum's own page: "Eine 300 bps
- V.21 Verbindung kann zwar fast jedes Modem herstellen, hat aber weder
Datenkompression noch Fehlerkorrektur." Nothing on the page mentions Bell
103, and a German-firmware modem answering a German board would not offer
it, so this is a pure V.21 target: we originate on channel 1 (980/1180)
and it answers on channel 2 (1650/1850). That makes it the one number in
this document that tests a code path bbslist.txt has never touched.

One thing to fix before dialling it: the sweep decided a line had picked
up by listening for 2100 Hz or 2225 Hz, and a board that answers V.21
without sending ANS first sends neither. Its channel 2 mark idles at
1650 Hz, so that frequency is now in AnswerWatch.FREQS in bbs-sweep.py;
without it this call would have been scored as no answer whatever came
back.

SnobSoft is a Commodore 64 board in Hamburg. A caller reached it from
Australia at 300 baud over VoIP on both ends in November 2025, which
settles the question of whether 300 bit/s survives two ATAs and 13000 km.

TECS has been reached with an acoustic coupler at 300 baud and also
answers faster modes.

8. Dead ends
-------------

The textfiles.com BBS list is an archive of numbers from the 1980s and
90s, preserved for the area codes rather than for dialling.

817-844-6611 (Fort Worth time and temperature) and similar time lines are
voice announcers, not modems.

300baud.dev is a browser simulation of a Bell 103 board. Its numbers
(702-555-01xx) are fiction and there is no server behind them.

The 8-Bit Boyz BBS directory claims to cover dial-up but lists telnet
addresses only.

The European BBS List (ytm5.bplaced.net/european-bbslist), linked from
several German forums as the continental equivalent of the Telnet BBS
Guide, is dead: the live site returns a PHP fatal error on an undefined
mysql_connect, and the January 2025 Wayback snapshot captures only the
country menu, not the records behind it.

Elektron BBS also publishes a German mailbox list, but it was last
touched in December 2009. Its two other entries -- BodySnatcher BBS
0511-2708643 and PBSOUND HQ 030-47300910 -- are seventeen years stale and
should be treated as archaeology unless something else corroborates them.

nodelist.fidonet.cc now does publish downloadable nodelists covering
2026, but it is still unreachable from this machine: plain HTTP is
intercepted by a safebrowse interstitial and HTTPS fails the handshake
outright. The Z2DAILY surveyed in section 4 was fetched by hand instead.

pweck.com, cited by PhreakNet's own BBS page as "The BBS List," has
lapsed: the domain now resolves to a GoDaddy parking page.

PhreakNet publishes a 500-plus-entry "BBS Directory" PDF
(w2k.phreaknet.org/bbs/bbs.pdf). Its file metadata gives a creation date
of October 2018, it carries no per-entry last-verified date, and its
contents are recognizable as the mega BBS list that has circulated since
the 1990s -- large blocks of Twin Cities-area (612/651) boards, FidoNet
nodes, and other names that read as archaeology rather than a maintained
roster. A handful of entries do corroborate numbers already in this
document at older values -- Empire of the Dragon 303-679-0161, Reign of
Fire 931-494-9100, Xanadu 780-439-8364, Realm of Darkness 623-907-2858 --
which confirms the list is real but stale, not that anything else in it
still answers. Treat it like the textfiles.com list: good for area codes,
not for dialling.


9. PhreakNet / InterLinked BBS -- a live system the guide doesn't tag Dial-Up
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

bbs.phreaknet.org (InterLinked BBS, on Lightweight BBS software) is a
working phone-phreak community board with a real modem bank -- the page
says 23 modems -- and it is listed on the Telnet BBS Guide itself
(telnetbbsguide.com/bbs/phreaknet/), which is why it is worth recording
here: the guide tags that entry SSH and Telnet only, not Dial-Up, so it
never appeared in the filtered dial-up listing this document's section 1
is built from. A directory-style census of the guide's Dial-Up tag will
always miss it.

The BBS's own page gives three direct-dial lines by exchange and line
number but never states the area code on any page this survey reached:

    ...-270-2441   300 bps, 8N1 -- the modem line
    ...-270-2445   45.45 bps, US TDD
    ...-270-2450   50 bps, international TDD

("Report-A-Payphone" and a handful of other numbers published in
PhreakNet's own directory at portal.phreaknet.org/directory are DISA
gateways into their phone network, not the BBS's modem line, and are not
candidates here.) The page also repeats the same POTS-vs-VoIP caution
already familiar from this project's own calls: "POTS connections are
recommended but VoIP connections at 2400 baud also work well." Worth
tracking down the area code and dialling -- a community that already
reasons carefully about POTS versus VoIP on a real modem bank is a good
match for what modec is trying to test against -- but not yet a candidate
that can be called from what's on hand.
